Steps to obtain an open work permit in Canada

  1. home
  2. blog
  3. Steps to obtain an open work permit in Canada

Are you looking for the flexibility to work for any employer in any location across Canada? Unlike a standard (closed) work permit that ties you to one specific employer, an Work Permit (OWP) in Canada offers unparalleled freedom.

This permit is a crucial stepping stone for many immigrants, giving you the power to explore the Canadian job market and gain valuable work experience that can lead directly to Permanent Residence.

However, eligibility for an OWP is limited to specific categories. This comprehensive guide will break down exactly how to get an Open Work Permit in Canada, covering eligibility, the application process, and key things you must know to ensure a smooth journey.

What is an Open Work Permit (OWP)?

An Open Work Permit is a special type of work permit that is not employer-specific. This means you generally do not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) or a job offer to apply.

It provides maximum flexibility, but keep in mind that OWPs do have two minor restrictions:

  1. You cannot work for an employer who is listed as ineligible by the Canadian government.
  2. You cannot work in a job that is part of the adult entertainment industry.

Key Categories for an Open Work Permit in Canada

You must fall into a specific immigration stream to qualify for an OWP. The most common and high-demand categories include:

  1. Spouses and Common-Law Partners of Workers or Students

    2. This is one of the most popular streams! If your spouse or common-law partner is already in Canada, you may be eligible for an Open Work Permit:

    • Spouse/Partner of a Skilled Worker: Your spouse/partner must be working in an eligible skilled occupation (TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3) and generally hold a work permit valid for a minimum remaining period (e.g., 6 months or more).
    • Spouse/Partner of an International Student: Your spouse/partner must be a full-time student enrolled in a degree-granting program (like a Master's or Ph.D.) at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI).

  2. Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP) Applicants

    3. Recent graduates from eligible Canadian Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs) can apply for the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP).

    • This is a highly desirable OWP that allows you to work for up to three years, providing the critical Canadian work experience needed for most permanent residence programs.

  3. Bridging Open Work Permit (BOWP) Applicants

    4. If you have already submitted your application for Permanent Residence (PR) under a major economic stream (like Express Entry, Provincial Nominee Program, or Quebec Skilled Worker), you may be able to apply for a Bridging Open Work Permit (BOWP).

    • The BOWP allows you to maintain your legal status and continue working in Canada while your PR application is being processed.

  4. Other Special Public Policies and Programs

    5. Canada occasionally introduces special temporary programs, such as those for vulnerable workers, refugee claimants, or specific public policies (like the recent measures for Hong Kong residents or certain Temporary Resident to Permanent Resident pathways).

    • Professional Tip: Eligibility requirements for spouses and partners are frequently updated by IRCC. Contact us today to verify your specific situation based on the latest rules and your partner's National Occupational Classification (NOC) TEER level.

Step-by-Step OWP Application Process

The application for an OWP in Canada is primarily completed online through the Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) portal. Here is a general outline of the process:

  • Step 1: Determine Your Eligibility

    • Use the official IRCC website tool or consult with an authorized immigration representative to confirm you meet all the criteria for your specific OWP category (Spouse, PGWP, BOWP, etc.).

  • Step 2: Gather Required Documents

    • The necessary documentation varies by category, but typically includes:

    • Valid Passport and travel documents.
    • Proof of your current status in Canada (if applicable).
    • Proof of relationship (for spouse/partner OWPs).
    • For PGWP: Official transcript and a letter of completion from your DLI.
    • For BOWP: Proof of PR application submission (e.g., Acknowledgment of Receipt letter).
    • Digital photo and any forms required by IRCC.

  • Step 3: Complete the Application Forms

    • Fill out the necessary IRCC forms accurately. Be sure to select "Open Work Permit" where applicable. Accuracy is paramount—even small errors can lead to processing delays or rejection.

Don't Navigate the OWP Maze Alone

The path to an Open Work Permit in Canada is a fantastic opportunity, but the process can be complex, with constantly changing rules and specific documentation requirements. A single mistake could result in months of delay or a refusal that impacts your long-term PR goals.
